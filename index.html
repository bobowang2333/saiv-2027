Building AI We Can Trust

AI is becoming more capable every year, and making it safe matters more than ever. For instance, AI agents can find and exploit vulnerabilities at scale, launch cyber attacks of their own, or write code that hides subtle safety bugs and security flaws. As AI moves into the physical world, through autonomous vehicles, robots, and the systems behind critical infrastructure, unsafe behaviour can have real-world consequences.

SAIV 2027 brings together researchers in AI and formal methods, but also related fields such as cybersecurity, software engineering, programming languages, control theory, or robotics that work on aspects of AI verification, to tackle the AI safety challenge. Topics of interest include AI-assisted code generation and translation that is safe by construction and backed by formal proofs of correctness; autoformalisation, which turns informal requirements into precise, machine-checkable proofs; formal explainability, which provides rigorous, provably correct explanations of AI decisions; runtime monitoring and safeguarding, which detect and correct unsafe behaviour before it causes harm; robustness against attacks and data poisoning; world models, which give AI systems an explicit, analysable representation of their environment so that the consequences of their actions can be predicted and verified before they are taken; and safety assurance for physical AI and robotics systems. Underpinning all of this is the development of formal specifications that capture precisely what it means for an AI system to be safe, secure, and correct.
